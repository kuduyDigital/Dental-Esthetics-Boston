## Audience & journey (H)
- **Primary:** adults aged 40–75, Back Bay and Greater Boston professionals, with complex needs: implants, failing bridgework, full-mouth rehabilitation, or veneers redone. They choose by expertise more than convenience.
  - *Discovery:* a referral, or a search like "dental implants Boston" or "prosthodontist Boston".
  - *Evaluation:* "Is this a specialist? How experienced? What does it look like after?"
  - *Trust signals:* specialist credentials, academic roles, before/after cases, a calm process, real reviews.
  - *Objections:* cost and insurance, pain, time, and whether they need a specialist at all.
  - *Conversion:* call, or book a consultation.
- **Secondary:** cosmetic patients (whitening, veneers, aligners) and local family/general care.
- **Referring dentists (H):** the academic profile suggests referrals matter. A small "For referring dentists" line is worth considering.

**Limit:** I did not do a full visual audit of competitor sites (official URLs were not all confirmed). From the category convention (H), most use template layouts: a stock-smile hero, "Best dentist" superlatives, icon grids and chat widgets.

## Opportunity
1. Lead with **specialist authority**: "the prosthodontists who teach it". This is a credential that can be checked, which superlatives are not.
2. Make implants and full-mouth rehabilitation the hero offering, with cosmetic and general care supporting it.
3. Use a calm editorial design, which suits a 40–75 audience making a high-cost decision better than the busy template on the current site.
4. Fix the functional problems: broken hours, dead buttons, a cropped logo, two `h1`s, and an unclear booking path.
